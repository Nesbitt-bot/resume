## Overview

A public WashU mathematics and computer-science note archive with 551 documents across 18 course directories, searchable on the web and accessible to AI tools.

## Highlights

- Published 551 Markdown and MDX documents across 18 mathematics, computer-science, and supplemental course directories for WashU learners.
- Built the searchable Next.js and Nextra site with weekly Algolia indexing, direct GitHub feedback and editing, distributed deployment support, and MCP access for AI tools.
- Serves about 10 users per week; after graduation, maintenance focuses on correcting errors and typos rather than adding product features.

## Project narrative

### Situation

I had hundreds of mathematics and computer-science course notes that could help other WashU students, while the larger Notechondria system was not stable enough to serve them promptly. Waiting for the larger platform would have kept already useful learning material private.

### Task

Publish the material through a lightweight, searchable note-sharing site for WashU students who want additional mathematics and computer-science references, while keeping the archive open to corrections and reuse.

### Action

I built and maintain a Next.js and Nextra static site containing 551 Markdown and MDX documents across 18 course directories: nine mathematics areas, eight computer-science courses, and supplemental material. Weekly Algolia indexing provides search, while every page exposes GitHub editing and issue-feedback paths so readers can report or correct mistakes. I also added distributed deployment support and an MCP server through which AI tools can list, read, and search the notes.

### Result

The public archive at notenextra.trance-0.com serves about 10 users per week. Feature development ended after graduation, but I continue correcting errors and typos so the shared material remains usable rather than abandoning it when the course work ended.
