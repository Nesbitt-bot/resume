  ## Primary story cards

  ### MTA schema disagreement → Ownership / Earn Trust / Invent & Simplify

  **Situation:** A 25-person advertising-strategy project depended on synthetic data, but contributors were producing incompatible datasets and implementations without a shared data contract.

  **Decision:** Treat integration as the immediate problem, even though I had been assigned to one group and had no formal authority over the rest of the team.

  **Action:**
  - Asked active contributors what data and interfaces their groups needed.
  - Defined typed schemas, validation, manifests, storage adapters, and standardized MTA interfaces.
  - Documented and tested the contracts while coordinating integration discussions.

  **Result:** The active subgroup adopted the shared interfaces. The mentor selected my implementation as the project's most mature product and best match for the expected deliverable.

  **Useful for:** **Ownership** — took responsibility for a cross-team dependency. **Earn Trust** — learned the groups' constraints before defining shared contracts. **Invent & Simplify** — placed incompatible implementations behind common, extensible interfaces.

  **Follow-up hooks:** no formal authority; fixed seeds and configuration hashes; CSV, SQLite, PostgreSQL, and external-writer paths; contributors who preferred independent approaches remained outside the shared workflow.

  ### MTA uncertain requirements / modular simulator → Bias for Action / Ownership / Invent & Simplify

  **Situation:** Client data and identifying details were withheld, while the team still needed dependable inputs before attribution methods or an optimizer could be evaluated.

  **Decision:** Build a reproducible synthetic baseline immediately, but leave explicit extension points for later approved data, storage, and models.

  **Action:**
  - Delivered the first usable simulation baseline in two days.
  - Made it configuration-driven and deterministic with fixed seeds, typed schemas, validation, and dataset manifests.
  - Separated evaluation-only ground truth from model features and added storage and model interfaces.

  **Result:** The team gained a reproducible development baseline instead of waiting for unavailable client data. The maintained workspace reports 476 passing Python tests and 47 dashboard tests; development and mentor evaluation remain ongoing.

  **Useful for:** **Bias for Action** — created a reversible baseline under uncertainty. **Ownership** — solved the upstream dependency rather than limiting work to the assigned subgroup. **Invent & Simplify** — modularized future variation instead of guessing one final requirement.

  **Follow-up hooks:** why synthetic data was necessary; how leakage was structurally prevented; why this is a pre-production proof of concept rather than evidence of advertising lift.

  ### Mathematical research → Learn & Be Curious / Dive Deep

  **Situation:** My Freiwald research and senior honors thesis examined why random states in high-dimensional bipartite quantum systems are highly entangled with high probability.

  **Decision:** Build the mathematical chain needed to connect concentration inequalities, quantum entanglement, and Gromov's observable diameter rather than treating the result as a black box.

  **Action:**
  - Studied non-commutative probability, metric-measure spaces, high-dimensional probability, manifolds, and quantum information.
  - Presented intermediate work on non-commutative probability and concentration of measure.
  - Used Hayden's result to obtain estimates for the observable diameter of complex projective spaces in the honors-thesis presentation.

  **Result:** Completed the year-long reading and research path and presented the senior honors thesis to the Washington University mathematics department on March 26, 2026.

  **Useful for:** **Learn & Be Curious** — crossed several unfamiliar mathematical areas to understand one claim. **Dive Deep** — followed the result through its geometric and quantum-information details.

  **Follow-up hooks:** why concentration implies typical entanglement; observable diameter; the role of complex projective space; how the research scope evolved from the summer program to the thesis.

  ### Note site simplification → Customer Obsession / Invent & Simplify

  **Situation:** Hundreds of mathematics and computer-science notes could help other WashU learners, but the larger Notechondria system was not stable enough to publish them promptly.

  **Decision:** Release a lightweight static site instead of withholding useful material until the larger platform was ready.

  **Action:**
  - Published 551 Markdown and MDX documents across 18 course directories with Next.js and Nextra.
  - Added weekly Algolia search plus GitHub editing and issue-feedback paths.
  - Corrected a Math 4121 set-boundary definition after Nathan Zhou reported it through Piazza, expanded the nearby notation reference, and credited him.

  **Result:** The public archive serves about 10 users per week, although I remain its primary user. The correction path caught an error that was difficult to find through solo proofreading of the full archive.

  **Useful for:** **Customer Obsession** — made the archive usable and responded to a real reader correction. **Invent & Simplify** — chose the smallest stable publishing path instead of waiting for the larger system.

  **Follow-up hooks:** why broad solo proofreading was impractical; search and correction workflow; limits of the usage evidence; static site versus the larger Notechondria platform.
