## Customer Obsession

Customer Obsession means starting from the person who must use the result, then working backward instead of treating implementation as the finish line. In my experience, this means giving classmates an immediately useful learning resource instead of asking them to wait for a perfect platform, and designing reliability analysis from the manager's decisions and dashboard experience backward to the data, metrics, and detection components required to support them.

<details className="principle-example">
<summary>NoteNextra / Notechondria</summary>

**Situation:** Hundreds of course notes could help learners, but a larger publishing system was not stable enough.

**Task:** Give classmates a reliable resource immediately after class instead of making them wait months for a more fully featured application.

**Action:** Worked backward from learner access: published the existing notes as a lightweight static site, then added search, GitHub correction paths, and weekly indexing.

**Result:** The archive serves about 10 weekly users; a reader-reported Math 4121 error was corrected and credited.

[Read the project](/projects/notenextra-notechondria/)

</details>

<details className="principle-example">
<summary>Reliability Event Detection Case Study</summary>

**Situation:** A manager needed to review network reliability without labeled failures or time to reconstruct the meaning of low-level operational records.

**Task:** Optimize the manager's experience as the product's client by starting with the decisions and drill-downs the delivered dashboard needed to support, then work backward to the analytical components required underneath it.

**Action:** I defined the daily report and interactive dashboard first—dates, affected units, systemic status, candidate correlates, impact ranking, and location-level drill-down—then reverse-mapped each view to the station metrics, historical and peer baselines, statistical tests, factor-enrichment logic, diagnostics, and data transformations needed to make it auditable.

**Result:** The notebook delivered a manager-oriented review interface backed by traceable event diagnostics, while keeping client, dataset, and domain details within the required disclosure boundary.

[Read the project](/projects/reliability-event-detection-case-study/)

</details>

## Ownership

Ownership means accepting responsibility for the outcome beyond the narrow boundary of an assigned role. In the MTA Strategy Optimizer, I did not limit myself to the data-science assignment I entered with: I initiated conversations across departments, worked closely with algorithm teams, and analyzed the data model required by each layer so the overall system could integrate. In Chinese Groundbreakers, I similarly continued proposing events, maintaining resources, and helping students when senior leadership had less capacity.

<details className="principle-example">
<summary>MTA Strategy Optimizer</summary>

**Situation:** A 25-person project lacked shared data contracts while approved source data was unavailable.

**Task:** Take responsibility for dependable upstream data and cross-group integration, although both extended beyond my assigned data-science work.

**Action:** Proactively consulted other groups, especially algorithm contributors, mapped each layer's inputs and outputs, and implemented the shared schemas, validation, adapters, tests, and documentation.

**Result:** The active subgroup integrated around the shared interfaces; the mentor called this the most mature implementation and best match for expectations.

</details>

<details className="principle-example">

**Situation:** Club leaders had reduced capacity during their final semesters, and activity was declining.

**Task:** Keep the club useful through graduation, including website, media, and member support outside my assigned event-planning duties.

**Action:** Repeatedly identified time-sensitive gaps, proposed events, maintained content, requested missing media, attended nearly every event, and offered individual course help.

**Result:** The club continued serving more than 300 members, and course support helped 15 students plan schedules.

## Invent and Simplify

Invent and Simplify is not novelty for its own sake; it is finding a smaller, clearer system that removes recurring friction. My projects repeatedly move in this direction: I replaced incompatible data-generation work with one reproducible contract in the MTA project, and separated the immediately useful static NoteNextra archive from the more ambitious Notechondria platform so that complexity would not block delivery.

**Task:** Replace incompatible one-off datasets with the smallest reproducible contract that every active group could extend.

**Action:** Delivered a fixed-seed baseline in two days, then consolidated recurring integration needs into typed schemas, manifests, validation, storage adapters, tests, and one documented MTA interface.

**Result:** The active subgroup integrated around the interfaces; the mentor called this the most mature implementation and best match for expectations.

## Are Right, A Lot

I do not treat “right” as a fixed position owned by one person. People act under different objectives, constraints, risk tolerances, and evaluation metrics, so a decision that looks inappropriate under one objective may be coherent under another. For me, this principle means actively seeking those perspectives, identifying what each person is optimizing, and comparing alternatives across measures such as cost, reliability, learning value, and implementation risk before committing to a decision.

<details className="principle-example">
<summary>ATTPDRL</summary>

**Situation:** A trailer-truck parking task allowed several plausible research paths, but nonlinear dynamics, sparse success, and expensive exploration made every choice a trade-off.

**Task:** Choose and revise the research direction by comparing evidence across convergence, reliability, compute cost, and learning value—not by defending the original proposal.

**Action:** Tested PPO and DDPG, helped analyze DQN and reward ablations, simplified the task when evidence demanded it, and stopped broad search when each complex agent cost roughly seven GPU-hours.

**Result:** Straight driving converged while 90-degree steering did not after five million steps. Rather than forcing one definition of success, we used the contrast between paths and metrics to revise the scope and clarify what each approach could establish.

[Read the project](/projects/attpdrl/)

</details>

<details className="principle-example">
<summary>MTA Strategy Optimizer</summary>

**Situation:** Data-science, algorithm, dashboard, and other project groups were developing different layers with different assumptions about the data they needed and the outcomes they were optimizing.

**Task:** Understand what each group was optimizing before deciding on interfaces, rather than assuming my original data-science view was sufficient.

**Action:** Sought perspectives outside my group, analyzed each layer's inputs, outputs, and evaluation concerns, and used the common requirements to design shared schemas and extension points.

**Result:** The active subgroup integrated around a common contract, while the discussions exposed why teams made different choices under different metrics rather than reducing disagreement to one side being right.

[Read the project](/projects/mta-strategy-optimizer/)

</details>

## Learn and Be Curious

Learn and Be Curious describes how I have approached both formal study and practical infrastructure since high school: I start with a real limitation, explore tools outside what I already know, and keep rebuilding until the system fits the need. Mathematical research took me across probability, geometry, and quantum information; my personal cloud took me from NAS file sharing to FreeBSD jails, Nextcloud, Linux, Docker, portable volumes, and cross-platform services. The lasting motivation is not novelty alone, but the satisfaction of learning a technique by applying it to make daily work more capable and efficient.

<details className="principle-example">

**Situation:** A research path required connecting high-dimensional probability, geometry, and quantum information.

**Task:** Learn enough probability, geometry, and quantum information to reconstruct the argument behind typical entanglement rather than quote it as a black box.

**Action:** Studied across the three fields, worked through intermediate results, and presented both intermediate work and the final thesis.

**Result:** Completed the year-long path and presented the senior honors thesis on March 26, 2026.

</details>

**Action:** Taught myself FreeBSD jails, then Linux, Docker, volumes, networking, and migration by repeatedly rebuilding Nextcloud and related cross-platform services on changing hardware.

**Result:** The service now runs on my home network, lets me use and share files across platforms and with friends, and can be migrated between suitable Docker hosts in about 30 minutes. ## Hire and Develop the Best

For my experience, this principle is better understood as raising the capability of the people around me, since I have not yet owned a formal hiring process. I have guided newer participants in ROAR, helped students plan courses through Chinese Groundbreakers, and made team contributions easier through documentation and shared interfaces. The evidence is strongest in mentoring and enablement, not hiring decisions.

**Result:** The active subgroup adopted the shared interfaces; the mentor selected my implementation as the project's most mature product and best match for expectations.

Constraints often force me to make the system simpler and more transferable.

My strongest examples have concrete endpoints: a deployed archive, a playable game, an integrated data contract, a daily reliability report, or a migration that can be repeated in about 30 minutes. I also distinguish delivery from success when a pipeline runs but the scientific or operational result is still inadequate.
